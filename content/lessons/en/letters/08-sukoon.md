## Screen 1: A letter with no vowel

{{2:80:8}} is read qul, say. The [[ل]] carries a small circle, and it is read with no vowel after it: the word stops on l.

That circle is the sukoon. A letter with a sukoon joins the vowel before it into one syllable.

## Screen 2: Short words with sukoon

Many short words end in a sukoon.

- {{2:74:30}}: min, from
- {{2:6:8}}: lam, not
- {{2:80:8}}: qul, say

## Check: The sukoon

question: How is a letter with a sukoon read?
- With a short a after it
- With no vowel after it
- Held for two counts
answer: 2

## Answer: No vowel

The sukoon closes the syllable: {{2:74:30}} is read min.

## Check: Reading {{2:6:5}}

question: How is {{2:6:5}} read?
- ʿa-lay-him
- ʿa-lī-hi-mi
- ʿa-la-ya-him
answer: 1

## Answer: ʿa-lay-him

Two letters carry a sukoon: [[ي]] glides after the fatha, and [[م]] ends the word.
