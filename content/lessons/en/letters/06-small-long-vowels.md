## Screen 1: A small alif above the letter

A small upright alif above a letter makes a long ā: the vowel is held for two counts instead of one.

{{1:4:1}} is read māliki, Master: the small alif above [[م]] makes mā. In {{2:7:9}}, ghishāwatun, a covering, it sits above [[ش]].

## Screen 2: Small yaa and small waaw

A small yaa after a letter with a kasra makes a long ī, and a small waaw after a letter with a damma makes a long ū. Both are held for two counts, like the small alif.

- {{83:17:6}}, bihī: the small yaa after [[ه]] with a kasra
- {{112:4:3}}, lahū: the small waaw after [[ه]] with a damma
