## Screen 1: The Arabic letters

Arabic has 29 letters, counting hamza. Arabic is read from right to left, so a line of the Quran starts at the right edge of the page.

letters: [[ا]] alif; [[ب]] baa; [[ت]] taa; [[ث]] thaa; [[ج]] jeem; [[ح]] ḥaa; [[خ]] khaa; [[د]] daal; [[ذ]] dhaal; [[ر]] raa; [[ز]] zaay; [[س]] seen; [[ش]] sheen; [[ص]] ṣaad; [[ض]] ḍaad; [[ط]] ṭaa; [[ظ]] ẓaa; [[ع]] ʿayn; [[غ]] ghayn; [[ف]] faa; [[ق]] qaaf; [[ك]] kaaf; [[ل]] laam; [[م]] meem; [[ن]] noon; [[ه]] haa; [[و]] waaw; [[ي]] yaa; [[ء]] hamza

The grid follows the order of a Qaida, the first reading book for the Quran. Most names begin with the sound of their letter: baa starts with b, meem with m.

## Screen 2: Letters told apart by dots

Many letters share one body and differ only in their dots. [[ب]] has one dot below, [[ت]] has two dots above and [[ث]] has three dots above.

- [[ب]] [[ت]] [[ث]]: one dot below, two above, three above
- [[ج]] [[ح]] [[خ]]: a dot inside, no dot, a dot above
- [[د]] [[ذ]]: no dot, a dot above
- [[ر]] [[ز]]: no dot, a dot above
- [[س]] [[ش]]: no dots, three dots above

## Screen 3: More letters told apart by dots

The same pattern runs through the rest of the alphabet. In each pair the second letter has the dot.

- [[ص]] [[ض]]: no dot, a dot above
- [[ط]] [[ظ]]: no dot, a dot above
- [[ع]] [[غ]]: no dot, a dot above
- [[ف]] [[ق]]: one dot above, two dots above

[[ف]] and [[ق]] also differ in their tail: the tail of [[ق]] dips below the line.

## Screen 4: Letters with their own shape

Nine letters, written alone, look like no other letter: [[ا]], [[ك]], [[ل]], [[م]], [[ن]], [[ه]], [[و]], [[ي]] and [[ء]].

[[ا]] alif is a single upright stroke. [[ل]] laam is also upright but curves to the left at the bottom. [[ن]] noon is a deep bowl with one dot above.

Yaa has two dots below. At the end of a word the Quran's script leaves them out: {{2:10:1}}, fī, in, ends in a yaa with no dots.

## Screen 5: Sounds English does not have

Nine letters have no English sound. A beginner learns them by ear from a teacher or a recording.

- [[ح]] ḥaa: a breathy h from the middle of the throat
- [[خ]] khaa: like ch in the Scottish word loch
- [[ع]] ʿayn: a tight sound from the middle of the throat
- [[غ]] ghayn: like a French r
- [[ق]] qaaf: a k said further back in the mouth
- [[ص]] [[ض]] [[ط]] [[ظ]]: heavy forms of s, d, t and dh, said with the back of the tongue raised

## Screen 6: The first letter of the Quran

{{1:1:1}} is the first word of the Quran. It starts on the right with [[ب]], the letter with one dot below.

## Check: Two dots above

question: Which letter has two dots above?
- [[ب]]
- [[ت]]
- [[ث]]
- [[ن]]
answer: 2

## Answer: The letter [[ت]]

[[ت]] taa has two dots above. [[ب]] has one dot below, [[ث]] has three dots above and [[ن]] is a bowl with one dot.

## Check: Reading direction

question: Where does a line of the Quran start?
- At the left edge of the page
- At the right edge of the page
answer: 2

## Answer: The right edge

Arabic is read from right to left, so every line starts at the right edge.

## Answer: The letter [[خ]]

[[خ]] khaa has a dot above. [[ج]] jeem has a dot inside and [[ح]] ḥaa has no dot.
