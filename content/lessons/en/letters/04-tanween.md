## Screen 1: A doubled vowel adds n

{{11:81:17}} is read aḥadun, one. Its last letter [[د]] carries two damma marks, and the two marks are read un.

A vowel mark written twice on the last letter of a word adds an n sound. This doubled vowel is called tanween.

## Screen 2: The three kinds of tanween

Each short vowel has a doubled form.

- Two fathas [[ـًا]]: an
- Two kasras [[ـٍ]]: in
- Two dammas [[ـٌ]]: un

The same word changes its ending with its place in the sentence: {{11:81:17}} aḥadun and {{2:102:27}} aḥadin.

## Screen 3: The alif after two fathas

Two fathas usually come with an [[ا]] after them. That alif is written but not read.

{{2:126:7}} is read baladan, a town. The final [[ا]] is silent; the word ends on an.

question: How does {{2:102:27}} end?

- in
- un
answer: 2

## Answer: in

The [[د]] carries two kasras, read in: aḥadin.

## Check: The silent letter

question: In {{2:126:7}}, which letter is written but not read?
- The first letter, [[ب]]
- The last letter, [[ا]]
answer: 2

## Answer: The final alif

After two fathas the [[ا]] only carries the vowel; the word is read baladan.

- un
answer: 3

## Answer: un

The [[د]] carries two dammas, read un: aḥadun, one.
