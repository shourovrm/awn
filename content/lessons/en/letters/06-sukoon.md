## Screen 1: A letter with no vowel

{{2:80:8}} is read qul, say. The [[ل]] carries a small circle, and it is read with no vowel after it: the word stops on l.

That circle is the sukoon. A letter with a sukoon joins the vowel before it into one syllable.

## Screen 2: Short words with sukoon

Many short words end in a sukoon.

- {{2:74:30}}: min, from
- {{2:6:8}}: lam, not
- {{2:80:8}}: qul, say

## Screen 3: Waaw and yaa with sukoon

A [[و]] or [[ي]] with a sukoon after a fatha makes a glide, not a long vowel.

{{1:4:2}} is read yawmi, day: the [[و]] has a sukoon after a fatha, so ya and w join into yaw.

With [[ي]] the glide is ay: {{106:1:2}} is read qurayshin, Quraysh.
