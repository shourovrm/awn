## Screen 1: Fatha

{{2:29:3}} is read khalaqa, he created. Each of its three letters carries a small slanted stroke above it, and each stroke is read as a short a.

That stroke is the fatha. [[بَ]] is read ba, [[تَ]] is read ta.

## Screen 2: Kasra

A kasra is the same small stroke written below the letter. It is read as a short i: [[بِ]] is read bi.

In {{6:54:16}}, ʿamila, he did, the middle letter [[م]] carries a kasra, so the word reads ʿa-mi-la.

## Screen 3: Damma

A damma is a small curl, like a tiny [[و]], above the letter. It is read as a short u: [[بُ]] is read bu.

{{3:144:13}} is read qutila, he was killed: damma on [[ق]], kasra on [[ت]], fatha on [[ل]].

## Screen 4: Reading letter by letter

A word with only short vowels is read one letter and its vowel at a time, from right to left.

- {{2:178:4}}: ku, ti, ba, so kutiba, it is prescribed
- {{2:29:3}}: kha, la, qa, so khalaqa

## Check: The vowel below

question: Which mark is read as a short i?
- The stroke above the letter (fatha)
- The stroke below the letter (kasra)
- The curl above the letter (damma)
answer: 2

## Answer: Kasra

The kasra sits below the letter and is read i, as in bi.

## Check: Reading {{2:178:4}}

question: How is {{2:178:4}} read?
- kataba
- kutiba
- kutubu
answer: 2

## Answer: kutiba

Damma on [[ك]], kasra on [[ت]], fatha on [[ب]]: ku-ti-ba.

answer: 1

## Answer: kha-la-qa

Each of the three letters carries a fatha: kha-la-qa, he created.

## Check: Reading {{86:5:4}}

question: How is {{86:5:4}} read?
- kha-la-qa
- khu-li-qa
- khi-lu-qa
answer: 2

## Answer: khu-li-qa

Damma, kasra, fatha: khu-li-qa, he was created. The letters are the same as in {{96:2:1}}; only the vowels change the meaning.
