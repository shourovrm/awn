## Screen 1: The sukun mark

The small circle on a letter, [[بْ]], is the sukun. A letter with a sukun has no vowel of its own, so it is read together with the letter on its right: [[اَبْ]] is ab, [[اِبْ]] is ib, [[اُبْ]] is ub.

reveal: [[اَعْ]] aʿ; [[اِعْ]] iʿ; [[اُعْ]] uʿ; [[اَغْ]] agh; [[اِغْ]] igh; [[اُغْ]] ugh; [[اَفْ]] af; [[اِفْ]] if; [[اُفْ]] uf; [[اَكْ]] ak; [[اِكْ]] ik; [[اُكْ]] uk; [[اَلْ]] al; [[اِلْ]] il; [[اُلْ]] ul; [[اَمْ]] am; [[اِمْ]] im; [[اُمْ]] um; [[اَنْ]] an; [[اِنْ]] in; [[اُنْ]] un; [[اَهْ]] ah; [[اِهْ]] ih; [[اُهْ]] uh; [[اَءْ]] aʾ; [[اِءْ]] iʾ; [[اُءْ]] uʾ

## Screen 2: Reading a letter with sukun

A letter with sukun leans on the letter before it, which must have a vowel: [[مَسْ]] is mas, [[بِلْ]] is bil.

## Screen 3: Qalqalah: five letters that bounce

Five letters, [[ق]] [[ط]] [[ب]] [[ج]] [[د]], give a small bounce when they have a sukun. The bounce is called qalqalah.

tip: Teachers join the five into a phrase to remember them: [[قُطْبُ جَدٍّ]], quṭbu jaddin.

reveal: [[اَقْ]] aq; [[اِقْ]] iq; [[اُقْ]] uq

The bounce also comes inside words: [[يَجْعَلُ]] is yajʿalu, with the bounce on the jīm.

reveal: [[فَلَقْ]] falaq; [[يَقْرَءُ]] yaqraʾu; [[قَدْرٌ]] qadrun; [[اَدْرَكَ]] adraka; [[اَبْتَرُ]] abtaru; [[فَاقْبَلَ]] faqbala; [[اَطْعَمَهُمْ]] aṭʿamahum
