## Screen 1: Three madd letters

Madd means stretching. An alif after a fatha, a wāw with sukun after a damma and a yā with sukun after a kasra stretch the vowel before them to two counts: [[بَ]] is ba, [[بَا]] is bā.

tip: The word [[نُوحِيهَا]] holds all three, each after its own vowel: wāw after a damma, yā after a kasra, alif after a fatha.

reveal: [[هَادَ]] hāda; [[مَاتَ]] māta; [[عَابَ]] ʿāba; [[نَامَ]] nāma

## Screen 2: Wāw and yā, and the standing marks

A wāw with sukun after a damma stretches the damma: [[بُوْ]] is bū.

reveal: [[فُومِ]] fūmi; [[كُونِ]] kūni; [[تُورِ]] tūri; [[نُورٌ]] nūrun; [[طُورٌ]] ṭūrun; [[حُورٌ]] ḥūrun; [[حُوتٌ]] ḥūtun

words:
- {{2:23:16}} | dūni

A yā with sukun after a kasra stretches the kasra: [[بِيْ]] is bī.

reveal: [[أَبِيْ]] abī; [[أَخِيْ]] akhī; [[دِيْنٌ]] dīnun; [[فِيْهِ]] fīhi; [[قِيْلَ]] qīla; [[نُرِيْ]] nurī

words:
- {{4:97:8}} | fīma
- {{12:94:8}} | rīḥa

A standing fatha [[ٰ]], a standing kasra [[ٖ]] or an upside-down damma [[ٗ]] stretches the letter the same way, with no madd letter written.
