## Screen 1: Alif makes a long a

{{2:30:2}} is read qāla, he said. The fatha on [[ق]] is followed by [[ا]], and together they are read as a long ā, held for two counts.

A short vowel lasts one count; a long vowel lasts two.

## Screen 2: Yaa and waaw make long i and u

The same happens with the other two vowels.

- Kasra then [[ي]]: a long ī, as in {{2:11:2}}, qīla, it is said
- Damma then [[و]]: a long ū, as in {{2:8:4}}, yaqūlu, he says

These three letters, [[ا]], [[و]] and [[ي]], are the long-vowel letters.

## Screen 3: Alif written as yaa

At the end of some words the long ā is written with a yaa shape without dots, [[ى]].

{{2:5:2}} is read ʿalā, on. The [[ى]] at the end is read as alif.

## Check: Long or short

question: Which word has a long vowel?
- {{2:30:2}}
- {{2:5:2}}
- Both
answer: 3

## Answer: Both

{{2:30:2}} has ā written with [[ا]], and {{2:5:2}} has ā written with [[ى]].

## Check: The long u

question: Which letters make a long ū?
- Fatha then [[ا]]
- Kasra then [[ي]]
- Damma then [[و]]
answer: 3

## Answer: Damma then waaw

{{2:8:4}}, yaqūlu, has a damma on [[ق]] followed by [[و]].
