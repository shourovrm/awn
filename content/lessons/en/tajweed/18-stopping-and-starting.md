## Screen 1: Stopping on a word

To stop on a word, drop its last vowel and give the last letter a sukoon.

- {{112:1:4}}: aḥadun becomes aḥad
- {{113:1:4}}: al-falaqi becomes al-falaq

## Screen 2: Endings that change at a stop

- Two fathas become a long ā: {{78:13:3}}, wahhājan, is read wahhājā at a stop.
- A round taa, [[ة]], becomes h: {{2:7:9}}, ghishāwatun, is read ghishāwah at a stop.

A word that already ends in a sukoon or a long vowel stays as it is.

## Screen 3: The joining alif

Many words start with an alif that carries a small mark like a ṣ, [[ٱ]]. It is a joining alif, hamzat al-waṣl: in the middle of reading it is silent and the word joins the one before. It is read only when you start with that word.

- Before the l of "al": start with a: {{1:2:1}}, al-ḥamdu
- Otherwise, look at the third letter. If it carries a damma, start with u: {{2:58:3}}, udkhulū. If not, start with i: {{1:6:1}}, ihdinā.
- A few nouns always start with i, whatever their third letter: [[ٱسْم]] ism, [[ٱبْن]] ibn, [[ٱمْرَأَة]] imraʾah.

## Answer: ghishāwah

At a stop a round taa becomes h.

## Check: Starting

question: How do you start {{1:6:1}}?
- ahdinā
- ihdinā
- uhdinā
answer: 2

## Answer: ihdinā

The third letter, [[د]], carries a kasra, so the joining alif is read i.
