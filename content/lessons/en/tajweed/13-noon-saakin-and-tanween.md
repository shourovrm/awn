## Screen 1: Four ways to read a silent noon

A noon saakin is a [[ن]] with no vowel, as in {{79:40:2}}, man. Tanween ends a word with the same silent n sound: {{2:7:11}} is read ʿadhābun. Both follow one of four rules, chosen by the letter that comes next.

- Clear (izhar): before the six throat letters
- Merged (idgham): before [[ي]] [[ر]] [[م]] [[ل]] [[و]] [[ن]]
- Changed to m (iqlab): before [[ب]]
- Hidden (ikhfa): before the remaining 15 letters

## Screen 2: Clear

Before [[ء]] [[ه]] [[ع]] [[ح]] [[غ]] [[خ]], the n is said clearly, with no nasal hold.

- {{79:40:2}} man khāfa: n before [[خ]]
- {{2:7:11}} ʿadhābun ʿaẓīm: n before [[ع]]

## Screen 3: Merged

The n disappears into the next letter, which is then doubled.

- With a nasal hold of two counts, before [[ي]] [[ن]] [[م]] [[و]]: {{2:8:3}}, may-yaqūlu; {{78:30:2}}, falan-nazīdakum
- Without a nasal hold, before [[ل]] [[ر]]: {{2:5:4}}, mir-rabbihim; {{2:2:6}}, hudal-lil-muttaqīn

Merging happens only between two words. Inside one word the n stays clear, as in dunyā.

## Screen 4: Changed to m

Before [[ب]] the n becomes an m, held through the nose for two counts. The Mushaf marks it with a small [[م]].

- {{86:7:2}}, mim-bayni
- {{2:10:9}}, alīmum-bimā

## Screen 5: Hidden

Before the other 15 letters the n is neither clear nor merged: the tongue does not touch for the n, and the nasal sound is held for two counts while the mouth gets ready for the next letter.

- {{2:4:8}}, min qablika
- {{79:37:2}}, man ṭaghā
- {{79:18:5}}, an tazakkā

question: Which rule applies to the n before [[ب]]?
- Clear
- Merged
- Changed to m
answer: 3

## Answer: Changed to m

Before [[ب]] the n becomes an m, held for two counts.

## Check: Merged without nasal sound

question: Before which letters is the n merged with no nasal hold?
- [[ل]] and [[ر]]
- [[م]] and [[ن]]
- [[ي]] and [[و]]
answer: 1

## Answer: [[ل]] and [[ر]]

Before [[ي]] [[ن]] [[م]] [[و]] the merge keeps a nasal hold; before [[ل]] and [[ر]] it does not.
