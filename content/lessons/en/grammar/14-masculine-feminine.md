## Screen 1: The round taa

{{30:33:13}}, raḥmatan, means mercy. Every Arabic noun is masculine or feminine. The round taa, [[ة]], at the end is the usual sign of a feminine noun. A few nouns are feminine with no sign, such as the sky and the sun.

words: ending
- {{30:33:13}} | raḥmatan · mercy | 4
- {{101:1:1}} | al-qāriʿah · the Striking Calamity | 7
- {{2:7:9}} | ghishāwatun · a covering | 4
- {{82:1:2}} | as-samāʾ · the sky
- {{81:1:2}} | ash-shams · the sun

## Screen 2: Verbs agree

A verb takes a feminine ending when a woman or a feminine noun does the action: {{66:11:9}}, qālat, means she said.

words: ending
- {{66:11:9}} | qālat · she said | 4
- {{97:5:2}} | hiya · she

## Check: Feminine

question: Which word is feminine?
- {{2:2:2}}
- {{30:33:13}}
answer: 2

## Answer: Raḥmah

{{30:33:13}} ends in [[ة]], so it is feminine. {{2:2:2}}, the Book, is masculine.
